# Getting started

This chapter introduces the core shape of a Gecode model: a space owns variables, propagators, and branchers; a search engine explores copies of that space. The web edition keeps the short route through the material, while the print stylesheet preserves a continuous reading path.

<h2 id="first-model">A first Gecode model</h2>

The running example is **SEND + MORE = MONEY**. Each letter denotes a distinct digit and neither leading letter may be zero. In positional notation, the central constraint is

$$
1000S + 100E + 10N + D + 1000M + 100O + 10R + E
= 10000M + 1000O + 100N + 10E + Y.
$$

<Figure id="fig-space" number="1.1" caption="A model space packages variables with the actors that constrain and branch on them.">
  <img src={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL}figures/space.svg`} alt="A model space containing variables, propagators, and branchers, connected to a search engine." />
</Figure>

As <XRef target="fig-space" label="Figure 1.1" /> shows, propagation and branching belong to the space; search orchestrates cloning and exploration. This separation is also the basis for Gecode’s efficient propagation control [@SchulteTack2008].

<Callout title="Space& versus Home">
Constraint-posting functions expose `Home`, a lightweight handle to the current space. When modeling, read it as “the space where this actor belongs”; the distinction becomes important when implementing propagators and branchers.
</Callout>

The coefficient arrays encode the displayed equation, `distinct` posts the global all-different constraint, and the brancher chooses a smallest-domain variable before trying its smallest value. Search uses `DFS` as the complete baseline.
